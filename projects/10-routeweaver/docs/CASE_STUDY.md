# Safe planning across a vehicle breakdown

RouteWeaver combines physical delivery constraints with versioned operational state. A cheap route is useful only if it can actually be dispatched. The reference validator reconstructs every directed arc, load, service-start window, shift and return, then checks the complete remaining-job partition and frozen commitments. Tiny exhaustive instances include unassigned deliveries and empty routes; analytical examples check both enumeration and evaluation. The current bounded correctness run passed 54 methods, including 14 real PostgreSQL methods, and killed all 82 selected Validator mutants. Those checks do not prove optimality for large instances.

Mutable Timefold objects stay inside the planning adapter. The original deterministic insertion and directed relocate/swap/2-opt stages produce immutable candidates; the independent validator checks every retained result against the same input hash. Reversing a segment in an asymmetric matrix changes every internal directed arc, so the original move evaluator recomputes complete route semantics instead of using a symmetric four-edge shortcut.

A breakdown changes the physical problem rather than merely its preferred ordering. Completed deliveries remain immutable history, onboard cargo remains on its actual vehicle, and new urgent demand stays at the depot. Failed commitments can be released only by an audited revision; undelivered failed-vehicle cargo remains visibly STRANDED_CARGO. The real 60-job/six-vehicle demonstration exercised these rules and rejected stale publication. The final-source lifecycle made 101 assertions, and real Chromium checked ten dispatcher behaviors, including the compact 107-character publication status. Earlier actual SIGKILL recovery retained a valid incumbent and advanced the fencing generation from 1 to 3; its historical source identity remains explicit.

PostgreSQL is the authority for worker generation and publication. Candidate and terminal writes lock the job and recheck its current state, database-clock lease and fence. Publication separately compares active input version and plan revision. Cancellation and completion serialize at those boundaries; a useful retained result cannot silently install itself. A later provenance audit also found that a new binary could recover work under an old submitted source label. The repaired dispatcher fails incompatible queued/recovered jobs before planning, preserving historical incumbents without producing mislabeled new candidates. This conservative policy also rejects UI-only source upgrades; its availability tradeoff is documented rather than hidden.

The completed quiet reference run measured ten declared synthetic inputs three times each, from 100 jobs/five vehicles to 500 jobs/25 vehicles, plus six JMH method/size rows with three forks each. All 30 results had zero hard violations and no same-input score regression; 21 served all demand and nine explicitly retained unmet demand. All 1,483 score/time trace points were ordered and nondegrading. On the preregistered improvable fixture, baseline operating score 101,101 became 96,106 after original moves and 90,120 after Timefold in every run: a 10.8614158% combined gain with identical assigned demand. This meets the proposed goal on that fixture, not on arbitrary routing data.

The limits are equally useful. The 500-job overconstrained first durable baseline consumed the whole cooperative budget in all three runs, so Timefold never started for that instance and the partial baseline remained unchanged. The longest worker duration was 31.552836 seconds against a configured 30-second budget; complete validation and database operations are not interrupted mid-operation. Six other outcomes reached the actual 100,000-step cap early and were not padded. Maximum observed service RSS was 937.91 MiB, including native/JIT/thread memory beyond the 768 MiB heap cap. The full three-run variability, queue/worker/baseline boundaries, allocation, GC and hashes are published in [performance](PERFORMANCE.md) and the [sealed packet](../evidence/performance-v1/packet.json). Three outcomes do not justify tail-latency percentiles.

The immutable model, reference validator/oracle, directed move evaluation, residual lifecycle, concurrency protocol and workload are original project work. [Timefold Solver 2.7.1](https://github.com/TimefoldAI/timefold-solver) supplies the improvement engine and planning API, and [OpenJDK JMH 1.37](https://github.com/openjdk/jmh) supplies the benchmark harness, statistics and GC profiler. Spring Boot, PostgreSQL, Jackson and Temurin/OpenJDK supply infrastructure. The measured gain belongs to the combined pipeline; neither those libraries nor their algorithms are claimed as original work. Timefold's PHASE_ASSERT mode was a service-solver setting; the standalone JMH methods measure validator/import work and do not run that solver.

All travel and demand are fictional. Release 1 has a common declared depot, observed stop-boundary replanning and no cargo transfer or mid-road interpolation. There is no customer deployment, production-readiness or large-instance optimality claim. Independent review verified the sealed packet and documentation within that measurement scope. Fresh-checkout, repository-wide CI, and release certification remain separate.
